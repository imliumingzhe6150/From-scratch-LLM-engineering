## Batch-Size Experiment Handoff

- Completed logged runs for batch sizes 1, 8, 32, 64, and 128 on MPS, plus
  two-step memory smoke tests for 128 and 256.
- Measured final logged throughput of 8,780 tokens/s for batch 1 and roughly
  10,475--10,732 tokens/s for batches 8 through 128. Batch 256 collapsed to
  1,542 tokens/s, making batch 128 the practical maximum tested.
- Audited the saved configurations before drawing conclusions. Batch 1 and 8
  each processed 3,276,800 training tokens, but batches 32, 64, and 128 all
  retained 1,600 steps and therefore processed 13.11M, 26.21M, and 52.43M
  tokens. Their validation sample sizes also increased with batch size.
- Because token budgets differ, the lower final losses at larger batch sizes
  do not isolate a batch-size effect. The existing logs remain valid for
  systems throughput, memory pressure, and fixed-update analysis.
- The next conversation should first choose between two honest paths: analyze
  the existing runs as a fixed-update experiment with the token-budget
  confound made explicit, or rerun only batches 32, 64, and 128 for 400, 200,
  and 100 steps to complete the originally intended fixed-token comparison.
- If the fixed-token rerun is selected, use these scaled settings:
  batch 32 = 400 steps / 40 warmup / validation every 80 steps with 4 batches;
  batch 64 = 200 / 20 / every 40 with 2 batches; batch 128 = 100 / 10 /
  every 20 with 1 batch. In every case, end cosine decay at the final step.
- After that decision, create batch-size learning curves and update the final
  Section 7.2 interpretation before moving to text generation.
